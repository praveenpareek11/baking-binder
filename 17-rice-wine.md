# 17. Rice Wine

**Program:** 17 — Rice Wine
**Loaf size:** Not selectable
**Crust color adjustable:** No
**Type:** Fermentation of glutinous rice into sweet rice wine (jiu niang / amazake)

---

## About this program

Ferments **steamed glutinous rice with distiller's yeast** into a lightly alcoholic, sweet rice wine. This is a multi-step process — the machine handles only the fermentation stage.

---

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glutinous rice | 500 g | |
| Water (for soaking) | 1500 g | For 16-hour soak |
| Distiller's yeast (rice wine yeast) | 1 tbsp / ~3 g | Different from baking yeast — buy "rice wine yeast" or "Chinese wine yeast" |
| Cool water (for mixing yeast) | 200 g | To dissolve the distiller's yeast |

---

## Method (per manual)

1. **Soak** 500 g cleaned glutinous rice in 1500 g water for **16 hours**, until you can crumble a grain between your fingers.
2. **Steam:** drain the rice. Line a steamer with gauze, place the rice in, and **high-heat steam for ~15 minutes.** Then cool to warm (~35°C) so the grains don't stick together.
3. **Mix yeast:** dissolve 5 g distiller's yeast in 200 g cool water; stir evenly. Combine with the warm rice.
4. **Transfer** the mixture to the bread pan, press and even out the surface. Start the **Rice Wine** program.
5. **Done when** you smell wine aroma — the fermented rice (jiu niang) is ready to enjoy.

---

## Tweak notes

- **Don't add the yeast to hot rice** — anything above 40°C will kill it. Cool to lukewarm first.
- **Make a depression in the center** of the rice in the pan — fermentation liquid pools there and you can taste-test progress.
- **Stronger / more alcoholic:** ferment longer (some recipes go 36–48 hours). The default program time is on the shorter side.
- **Sweeter:** ferment slightly less than program default — sugars haven't fully converted yet.
- **To stop fermentation:** refrigerate the finished product. It will keep slowly fermenting in the fridge, so use within ~1 week.
- **Safety:** use clean utensils and a clean pan — bad bacteria can cause off-fermentation.
