# 14. Yogurt

**Program:** 14 — Yogurt
**Loaf size:** Not selectable
**Crust color adjustable:** No
**Type:** Fermentation — holds milk at warm temperature for yogurt cultures to grow

---

## About this program

Ferments milk into yogurt by maintaining a warm temperature for several hours.

---

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk | 1000 ml (1 litre) | Whole milk gives thicker yogurt; UHT or pasteurized both work |
| Lactic acid bacteria (starter) | 100 ml | Plain live-culture yogurt with no additives, OR a commercial yogurt starter |

---

## Method

1. **Heat milk to ~85°C** in a separate pot, then cool to ~43°C (lukewarm). This step kills competing bacteria and denatures milk proteins for a thicker set. **Skip if using UHT milk.**
2. **Whisk in the starter** (the 100 ml of live-culture yogurt) until smooth.
3. Pour into the bread pan, lock in the machine, close the lid, and start Program 14.
4. After the program ends, transfer to clean jars and **refrigerate at least 4 hours** before eating — the yogurt continues to set in the cold.

---

## Tweak notes

- **For Greek-style yogurt:** strain the finished yogurt through cheesecloth or a fine sieve for 2–4 hours in the fridge to remove whey.
- **Sweetened/flavored:** add honey, vanilla, or fruit puree **after** fermentation (sugar can inhibit some cultures).
- **Reusing your own yogurt as starter:** works for about 5–6 generations before the culture weakens; then start fresh.
- **Plant-based:** soy or oat milk can work but result is much looser; coconut milk yogurt needs a thickener (1 tbsp tapioca starch per litre).
- **Make sure all utensils are very clean** — contamination is the most common cause of failed yogurt.
